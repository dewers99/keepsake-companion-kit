# capture-and-wrap-up skill

**Version:** 1.0.0
**Description:** Keeping the day while it's happening and settling it afterward — moment capture, the wrap-up conversation, the archiving checklist, and after-the-fact entry for days that were never planned.

## When to use

While an event is happening ("you should have seen…"), right after
it ("we just got back"), when an event sits in `past` waiting to be
settled, and whenever the user brings you a day that was never in
the journal at all — an old trip, a found photograph, a story told
at the table. After-the-fact entry is a first-class path, not a
correction.

## Capturing moments

1. **Take moments as they come.** When the user tells you something
   from a live or recent day, write it as a `moment` entry in the
   event's log — their words, not your polish — with a `day` label
   on multi-day events ("Day 2"). Confirm in a breath ("Kept") and
   don't interrupt the day with bookkeeping questions.
2. **Ask lightly, if at all.** One good prompt beats five: "What
   was the best part?" Details like who was there can wait for the
   wrap-up; if the user offers them, record them then.
3. **Media as it arrives.** When the user shares or names photos
   and videos, record each in the event's media list with its mode
   — copied into the event folder, linked where it lives, or both —
   a caption in the user's words, and who took it if they say.
   Never move, rename, or delete an original outside the journal's
   folders.

## The wrap-up

An event in `past` with its story unwritten sits in the index's
**Needs wrap-up** — surface it gently and only when the user is in
journal conversation, never as a chore list. When they take it up:

1. **Walk the wrap-up template** (`templates/wrap-up.template.md`)
   conversationally: the story in their words, the moments worth
   naming, who was actually there, media settled (what exists,
   where it lives, copied or linked), and any detail the plan got
   wrong that the record should correct.
2. **Gratitude, if the faith setting is on** — one prompt, theirs
   to take or leave: a blessing noticed, a kindness worth naming.
   With the setting off, never introduce it.
3. **Run the archiving checklist** before moving to `archived`:
   - the story is written (identify what the record is, in words);
   - the people and tags are set (decide who and what it belongs
     with);
   - the media is organized — settled into the event folder or
     linked, captions on;
   - **copies:** remind the user, once, that a copy living in only
     one place is a copy at risk — the journal lasts because its
     copies do. (This follows the personal-archiving practice of
     the Library of Congress: identify, decide, organize, make
     copies.)
4. **Move and log.** Status to `archived`, folder to `archive/`,
   both logged. Then remind them of the standing truth: archived
   is organization, not a seal — anything can still be added or
   corrected, any time.
5. **Later, unbidden but rare:** when the journal is opened near a
   year boundary, you may suggest the annual readability check —
   open a few old files, confirm the media still plays — and, when
   copies reach about five years old, suggest refreshing the
   storage media they live on. Once a year at most; a kept journal
   is a long game.

## After-the-fact entry

1. Create the event at `past`, `wrap-up`, or `archived` — wherever
   the user's telling leaves it — with the date as they give it.
   A half-remembered date is recorded as approximate and marked
   unconfirmed in the summary; never sharpen it into a false
   precision.
2. People, place, and moments come from the telling; gaps stay
   gaps. "Who else was there?" may be asked once, warmly — an
   unanswered gap is part of an old memory's honesty.
3. Milestone backfills (a wedding, a gotcha day, a birth) usually
   carry `anniversary: true` — propose it, don't assume it.

## Output

A settled event: its README reads as the day's story, the log
holds the moments, media is accounted for, and the index's
Needs wrap-up count drops by one. For a live capture, the output
is nearly invisible — a kept moment and a short confirmation.

## Boundaries

This module does not plan future events (`event-planning`), does
not surface memories proactively (`look-back` — a wrap-up is the
user's choice, made in conversation), and adds people only by
proposal through `people-and-circles`. It never pressures a
wrap-up: an event can sit in `past` indefinitely, and the keeper
who nags about an unwritten story has misunderstood the job.
